3. Types of Questions

Research always starts with a question. But not all questions are good for research.

Closed Questions

Simple questions. Short answers.

Open Questions

Big questions. Long answers. (Good for research!)

Closed Questions

  • There is only ONE right answer.
  • The answer is a simple fact or number.
  • You can find the answer in 10 seconds on Google.
  • Bad for a big research project.

Examples:

"What color is a banana?" (Yellow)

"How many legs does a spider have?" (Eight)

Open Questions

  • There is MORE THAN ONE answer.
  • People can have different opinions (say Yes or No).
  • You must read a lot to explain your answer.
  • Good for a big research project!

Examples:

"Why do bananas turn black?" (Needs science to explain)

"Should we keep spiders as pets?" (People will argue yes/no)

5. Features of a Good Question

Look at Goal 4. What makes a question GOOD for research?

"Should we remove all sugar from our diet?"

1. It is Arguable

People will say "Yes" AND people will say "No". You can have a debate.

2. It requires Evidence

You must find facts (from doctors, scientists) to prove your point.

3. It is Clear

We know exactly what the question is asking (sugar in diet).

4. It is not Biased

It doesn't tell you the answer in the question. (e.g., "Why is sugar terrible?" is biased).

Making Questions Better

Sometimes a question is too simple (closed). How can we make it open and arguable?

Bad (Closed) Question:

"Are cars fast?"

Good (Open) Question:

"Should cars be banned in city centers?"
