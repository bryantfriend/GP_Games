Tip Time!

Read the orange 'Tip' box on the right side of your page.

It says effective research helps you find relevant information, facts/statistics, and check information.

Over the next 3 slides, we will break down exactly what these three things mean.

1. Finding "Relevant" Information

Relevant information means it directly answers your specific question.

Example:

If your research question is "What do polar bears eat?", an article about how fast polar bears can swim is interesting, but it is NOT relevant to your specific question.

2. Facts & Statistics

Facts

Statements that can be proven true with evidence.

"Water boils at 100°C."

Opinions

Personal feelings or beliefs. Not good for research evidence!

"Winter is the best season."

3. Checking Information (Cross-checking)

Never rely on just one source. Effective researchers check if multiple sources agree!

+

=

Verified Fact!

How else can we check a source?

Before we answer Question 3, let's learn the 3 W's of evaluating sources.

WHO

Who wrote this? Are they an expert or just a random person?

WHEN

When was this published? Is the information outdated or recent?

WHY

Why was this written? To inform you with facts, or to sell you something?
